The Broward market in one page

Fort Lauderdale, Pompano Beach, Plantation, Sunrise, Hollywood and Coral Springs sit inside a single competitive county market, and most carriers writing Miami-Dade write Broward too. What changes is which hospital systems and physician groups anchor each network tier, and that difference is the one that decides whether a plan actually works for your team.

Broward’s small-business base leans heavily on marine and yachting services, construction and the trades, hospitality, healthcare services, and a growing professional and tech sector along the I-95 corridor. Those are very different risk and roster profiles, and they should not be shopped the same way. A twelve-person marine electrical contractor and a twelve-person accounting firm will get meaningfully different answers from the same carrier panel.

Growing past the small-group ceiling

Broward has a lot of businesses moving through the 40-to-60 employee range, and that transition matters more than most owners realise. At 50 full-time-equivalent employees, the federal employer mandate begins to apply, and the calculation counts part-time hours in aggregate rather than heads — two half-time employees can add up to one FTE.

The number is measured on the prior calendar year, which means the mandate arrives a year after you cross the line, not the day you hire. That lag is an opportunity: an employer who sees the threshold coming has twelve months to structure coverage deliberately rather than scrambling to comply. Employers who do not see it coming find out with a penalty notice.

If you expect to cross 50 FTEs in the next eighteen months, the plan you buy now should be chosen with that in mind. Some structures scale cleanly across the threshold and some have to be torn up and rebuilt.

Marine, trades and seasonal rosters

The marine industry is Broward’s distinct staffing problem. Yards, refit shops, canvas and rigging outfits and yacht management companies run on a core of full-time staff plus a wide band of seasonal and project labour that swells for the season and thins out afterwards. Underwriters look at that census and see instability. It is quotable anyway — you just have to define eligibility around your permanent crew rather than everyone who worked a hull in March.

The same applies to the trades. Construction and specialty contractors in Broward often carry a stable office and supervisory core plus a variable field roster. Setting the eligibility threshold at 30 hours with a standard waiting period of 30 or 60 days keeps the plan anchored to the people who stay, keeps participation math clean, and stops short-tenure hires from churning the enrolment file every quarter.

For crews that are genuinely contractor-based rather than W-2, a group plan is the wrong tool. An ICHRA gives each person a fixed monthly allowance to buy their own individual coverage, which travels with them, costs you a predictable number, and carries no participation test.

What Broward employers pay in 2026

The KFF 2025 Employer Health Benefits Survey put average annual single-coverage premiums at $9,325 nationally, about $777 per employee per month, with small firms of 10 to 199 workers at $9,211.

For 2026 planning in Broward, mid-tier small-group coverage commonly runs roughly $450 to $740 per employee per month before employer contribution. Broward tends to quote a shade under Miami-Dade on comparable plans, though the gap is small enough that it should never drive a decision on its own. These are planning ranges — ages, plan tier and carrier make the real number.

Trades employers with younger crews are frequently the biggest beneficiaries of level-funded plans in this county. If your team skews under 40 and uses relatively little care, a level-funded arrangement often quotes below the fully insured equivalent and returns unused claims funds at the end of the year. It is not right for every group, and we will say so when it is not.

Checking the network before you sign

Broward employees commute north-south along I-95 and Federal Highway, and east-west across a county that is wider than it looks on a map. A network that covers Fort Lauderdale beautifully but thins out in Coral Springs or Miramar will fail a chunk of your staff quietly, and you will not hear about it until someone needs a specialist.

Before enrolment, we run a simple check: take the three or four practices your employees actually use, plus the hospital closest to where most of them live, and confirm each one against the exact network name printed on the quote. Carriers sell several networks under one brand, and the network name — not the carrier logo — is what determines access.

If you have been turned down before

Plenty of Broward small businesses have been told they do not have enough employees enrolling, or that they must contribute more of the premium than they can afford. Both of those are participation and contribution rules, and both are waived once a year.

Between 15 November and 15 December, fully insured small-group carriers must accept eligible small employers applying for a 1 January effective date, regardless of participation or contribution levels. All other underwriting rules and documentation requirements still apply, and carrier deadlines in that window are unforgiving. If you were declined earlier in the year, that is the month to come back.

What you need to get an accurate quote

Carriers cannot price a group from a headcount alone, and a quote built on estimates gets re-rated later, which is how employers end up with a number that moves between the proposal and the invoice. What underwriters need is short and specific.

A census: for each eligible employee, date of birth, home ZIP code, gender, and whether they will enrol as employee-only, employee plus spouse, employee plus children, or family. That is it — no names, no salaries, no medical history for a fully insured quote. For a level-funded quote, most carriers also ask each employee to complete a brief health questionnaire, which is what lets them price below the fully insured rate for a healthy team.

You will also need your legal business name and EIN, your business address, an indication of what percentage of the premium you intend to contribute, and your intended effective date. Most employers can pull all of that together in under an hour from payroll. If your payroll system exports a census file, we can usually work straight from it.

The four mistakes that cost Florida employers the most

Shopping on premium alone. The cheapest quote in the stack is usually the narrowest network, and a plan people cannot use conveniently gets abandoned. Compare the network name on the quote, not the carrier logo.

Assuming you must cover dependents. Carriers generally require the employer to fund at least half the employee-only premium. Dependent coverage can be entirely employee-paid, and structuring it that way is both common and compliant.

Counting waivers as declines. Employees covered through a spouse, a parent, Medicare, Medicaid or TRICARE are generally valid waivers and come out of the participation calculation. Groups fail participation on paper all the time when they would have passed once waivers were counted correctly.

Renewing on autopilot. Carriers do not reward loyalty with pricing. A renewal is a quote like any other, and the only way to know whether yours is fair is to put the same census in front of the rest of the panel at the same time.

How long the whole process takes

From first conversation to active coverage is usually two to four weeks, and most of that is waiting on people rather than on carriers. The shape of it is consistent: a short call to confirm eligibility and what you want to spend, one to three business days to assemble quotes across the panel, a review conversation, then enrolment.

Enrolment itself is the step that slips. Every eligible employee must either enrol or sign a waiver, and chasing the last two signatures reliably takes longer than everything before it combined. Carriers set hard cut-off dates ahead of each effective date — commonly around the 15th to the 20th of the month before — and missing one pushes you a full month.

The practical implication: if you want coverage effective on the first of next month, start now rather than in three weeks. If you are aiming at a 1 January effective date through the annual special enrolment window, start in October.
