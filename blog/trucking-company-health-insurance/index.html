Trucking

Health insurance for trucking and logistics companies

A plan that works at the terminal and fails on the road is the specific way trucking benefits go wrong. Network geography matters more here than in any other industry we place.

Your people are not where your business is

Every other employer can map a network against a metro. Trucking cannot. Your drivers live in different states than your terminal, and they need care in states neither of you planned for. A regional HMO priced beautifully for your home county is close to useless for a driver who gets sick in another state.

This is the one industry where paying up for a genuine nationwide PPO tier is usually the correct call rather than a luxury. UnitedHealthcare Choice Plus, Aetna Choice POS II, Cigna Open Access and the BlueCard network through a Blue plan are the tiers built for this. Check the actual network name on the quote, not the carrier logo — carriers sell narrow networks under the same brand.

The alternative that often works better: an ICHRA. Each driver takes a monthly allowance and buys a plan in the state where they actually live, with local network access. Your cost is fixed and predictable, and the geography problem disappears entirely.

Drivers, owner-operators and the classification line

W-2 company drivers are eligible employees and go on the group plan normally. Owner-operators running under their own authority generally are not — they are independent businesses, not your employees, and cannot be enrolled.

Fleets running a mix need to be careful here, because the classification question in trucking draws regulatory attention and the census you submit is a representation. Settle it with your CPA before quoting. For a fleet that is mostly owner-operators, an ICHRA or a well-structured association arrangement is the honest path.

One practical note: DOT physicals are a condition of employment, not a health plan benefit, and they are not covered as preventive care under a group medical plan. Some employers reimburse them separately. Worth budgeting for rather than discovering at renewal.

What trucking companies pay in 2026

The KFF 2025 survey puts average single coverage at $9,325 a year nationally, about $777 per employee per month.

Trucking generally quotes at or slightly above the middle for 2026, commonly $450 to $760 per enrolled employee per month before employer contribution. Two reasons: driver populations often skew older than construction or hospitality, and the nationwide network tier that trucking actually needs costs more than the regional tier a local employer can use.

That premium is usually worth paying. A cheaper narrow-network plan that drivers cannot use on the road produces exactly the outcome you were trying to avoid — people who resent the deduction and drop it at renewal.

Benefits and driver retention

Driver turnover is the industry’s defining cost problem, and pay alone has proven a weak lever because every competitor can match it within a week. Health coverage is stickier: it is harder to replicate, it covers families, and losing it is a real cost to the driver rather than an abstract one.

Fleets that offer coverage and communicate it clearly — what it covers, what it costs per week, how to use it on the road — consistently report it mattering more in recruiting conversations than the headline cents-per-mile.

What you need to get an accurate quote

Carriers cannot price a group from a headcount alone, and a quote built on estimates gets re-rated later, which is how employers end up with a number that moves between the proposal and the invoice. What underwriters need is short and specific.

A census: for each eligible employee, date of birth, home ZIP code, gender, and whether they will enrol as employee-only, employee plus spouse, employee plus children, or family. That is it — no names, no salaries, no medical history for a fully insured quote. For a level-funded quote, most carriers also ask each employee to complete a brief health questionnaire, which is what lets them price below the fully insured rate for a healthy team.

You will also need your legal business name and EIN, your business address, an indication of what percentage of the premium you intend to contribute, and your intended effective date. Most employers can pull all of that together in under an hour from payroll. If your payroll system exports a census file, we can usually work straight from it.

The annual window that waives participation rules

If a carrier has told you no because too few employees would enrol, or because you cannot meet the contribution minimum they demand, there is one route back in and it runs on a fixed calendar.

Every year between 15 November and 15 December, fully insured small-group carriers must accept eligible small employers applying for a 1 January effective date, regardless of participation or contribution levels. The two rules that block most small groups simply do not apply during that month.

Everything else still holds — you must be a legitimate small employer, documentation must be complete, and carrier deadlines inside the window are strict and not negotiable. If you were declined earlier in the year, that is when to come back, and October is when to start assembling the census rather than the first week of December.

How long the whole process takes

From first conversation to active coverage is usually two to four weeks, and most of that is waiting on people rather than on carriers. The shape of it is consistent: a short call to confirm eligibility and what you want to spend, one to three business days to assemble quotes across the panel, a review conversation, then enrolment.

Enrolment itself is the step that slips. Every eligible employee must either enrol or sign a waiver, and chasing the last two signatures reliably takes longer than everything before it combined. Carriers set hard cut-off dates ahead of each effective date — commonly around the 15th to the 20th of the month before — and missing one pushes you a full month.

The practical implication: if you want coverage effective on the first of next month, start now rather than in three weeks. If you are aiming at a 1 January effective date through the annual special enrolment window, start in October.
