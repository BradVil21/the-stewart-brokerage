Texas

Group health insurance in Houston, Dallas & Austin

Texas prices small-group coverage metro by metro. Here is how the big four markets differ in 2026, what employers are actually paying, and how to shop them properly.

By The Stewart Brokerage · 21 August 2026 · 8 min read

Ask what small business health insurance costs in Texas and you will get a useless answer, because Texas is not one market. Carriers file rates and build networks county by county, which means the same ten-person team can see meaningfully different premiums — and very different doctor lists — in Houston, Dallas, and Austin. If you shop your group like the state is one market, you leave money on the table.

Why your metro changes the price

Two forces set your local price: how many carriers compete for small groups in your county, and what the local hospital systems charge them. A metro with four or five carriers fighting for small-group business prices sharper than one with two. A metro dominated by a single hospital system prices higher, because the carrier has no leverage. This is why quoting every carrier in your county — not a statewide average — is the whole job.

Houston and Harris County

Houston is the deepest small-group market in the state: more competing carriers, more plan designs, and strong HMO options built around the major systems alongside statewide PPO networks. The spread between the cheapest quote and the best-value quote is routinely wide here, which cuts both ways — great if someone shops it for you, expensive if you take the first renewal that lands in your inbox. Energy, logistics, and medical-adjacent employers dominate our Houston book.

Dallas–Fort Worth

DFW behaves like two overlapping markets, and networks that look identical on paper can differ on the ground between Dallas and Tarrant counties. Check where your employees actually live before falling in love with a quote — a plan anchored to one side of the metroplex can leave the other side driving past three in-network hospitals to reach a fourth. Level-funded plans price notably well for younger DFW teams in professional services and the trades.

Austin and San Antonio

Austin skews young, and that shows up in level-funded quotes: healthy-team economics with money back in a good claims year. Tech employers with remote staff outside Texas usually pair a Texas plan with a nationwide PPO tier, or move to an ICHRA so each employee buys in their own state. San Antonio runs steadier — fewer carriers than Houston, but dependable network coverage and pricing that rewards early renewal shopping.

What metro employers pay

For 2026 planning, mid-tier small-group coverage in the big Texas metros commonly lands in the range of roughly $400 to $700 per employee per month before the employer contribution — with Houston and DFW usually sitting a notch above Austin and San Antonio for comparable plans. Treat these as planning ranges, not quotes: employee ages, ZIP codes, plan tier, and carrier appetite move the real number, and a well-shopped group frequently beats its first renewal offer by double digits.

How to shop it properly

Get every carrier writing in your county quoted on the same census, compare networks against where your people actually live, and run a level-funded quote alongside the fully-insured ones — for many Texas metros teams it is the sharpest price on the table.
