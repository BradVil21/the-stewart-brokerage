Nonprofits

Health insurance for small nonprofits

Nonprofits compete for staff against employers who can simply pay more. Benefits are the lever that closes that gap — and there is a tax credit most small nonprofits never claim.

The tax credit most nonprofits miss

The Small Business Health Care Tax Credit is available to tax-exempt organizations, not just for-profit companies, and it is refundable for nonprofits — claimed against payroll taxes on Form 990-T rather than income tax you do not pay.

The general conditions: fewer than 25 full-time-equivalent employees, average annual wages below an inflation-adjusted threshold, the employer paying at least 50 percent of employee-only premium, and coverage purchased through the SHOP marketplace. The credit is worth up to 35 percent of premiums for tax-exempt employers, available for two consecutive years.

The SHOP requirement is the part that trips organizations up, and the rules around SHOP availability have shifted over the years. Verify current eligibility with your accountant before building it into a budget — but do verify it, because for an organization at the right size it is real money that is routinely left unclaimed.

Grant-funded budgets and predictable cost

The particular problem for nonprofits is not the premium level, it is variance. Grant cycles are fixed and a mid-year renewal increase you did not budget for has nowhere to come from.

Two structures help. A fully insured plan with a 12-month rate guarantee gives you a number you can put in a grant application and defend for the year. An ICHRA gives you something even more predictable: you set the monthly allowance, and that allowance is your cost, full stop — it does not move at renewal unless you move it. For grant-funded organizations that certainty is often worth more than a slightly lower average premium.

You are also not required to fund dependent coverage. Covering employee-only generously and leaving dependents employee-paid is common and keeps your line item stable.

What small nonprofits pay in 2026

Nationally, the KFF 2025 survey put average annual single coverage at $9,325, about $777 per employee per month.

Small nonprofits commonly plan on $420 to $700 per enrolled employee per month for a mid-tier plan in 2026 before employer contribution. Staff age mix drives most of the variation — a youth-services organization and a clinical services organization will quote quite differently.

Run the tax credit against these numbers before deciding what you can afford. At 35 percent of premium for a qualifying tax-exempt employer, it changes the arithmetic materially for organizations under 25 FTEs.

Competing for staff without competing on salary

Nonprofits lose good people to employers who can pay 20 percent more. Matching that is usually impossible; changing the comparison is not. An employer-funded health plan is worth several thousand dollars a year to an employee, is not taxable income to them, and is something a surprising number of competing small employers still do not offer.

It also matters for the people you most want to keep — program staff in their thirties and forties with families, exactly the group for whom coverage outweighs a modest salary difference.

What you need to get an accurate quote

Carriers cannot price a group from a headcount alone, and a quote built on estimates gets re-rated later, which is how employers end up with a number that moves between the proposal and the invoice. What underwriters need is short and specific.

A census: for each eligible employee, date of birth, home ZIP code, gender, and whether they will enrol as employee-only, employee plus spouse, employee plus children, or family. That is it — no names, no salaries, no medical history for a fully insured quote. For a level-funded quote, most carriers also ask each employee to complete a brief health questionnaire, which is what lets them price below the fully insured rate for a healthy team.

You will also need your legal business name and EIN, your business address, an indication of what percentage of the premium you intend to contribute, and your intended effective date. Most employers can pull all of that together in under an hour from payroll. If your payroll system exports a census file, we can usually work straight from it.

The annual window that waives participation rules

If a carrier has told you no because too few employees would enrol, or because you cannot meet the contribution minimum they demand, there is one route back in and it runs on a fixed calendar.

Every year between 15 November and 15 December, fully insured small-group carriers must accept eligible small employers applying for a 1 January effective date, regardless of participation or contribution levels. The two rules that block most small groups simply do not apply during that month.

Everything else still holds — you must be a legitimate small employer, documentation must be complete, and carrier deadlines inside the window are strict and not negotiable. If you were declined earlier in the year, that is when to come back, and October is when to start assembling the census rather than the first week of December.

How long the whole process takes

From first conversation to active coverage is usually two to four weeks, and most of that is waiting on people rather than on carriers. The shape of it is consistent: a short call to confirm eligibility and what you want to spend, one to three business days to assemble quotes across the panel, a review conversation, then enrolment.

Enrolment itself is the step that slips. Every eligible employee must either enrol or sign a waiver, and chasing the last two signatures reliably takes longer than everything before it combined. Carriers set hard cut-off dates ahead of each effective date — commonly around the 15th to the 20th of the month before — and missing one pushes you a full month.

The practical implication: if you want coverage effective on the first of next month, start now rather than in three weeks. If you are aiming at a 1 January effective date through the annual special enrolment window, start in October.
